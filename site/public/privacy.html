Privacy

Chord has no account and sends no telemetry: it never reports what you do or where you go. This page lists everything it contacts on its own, why, and how to turn each one off. The sites you visit see your visits, as in any browser.

What stays on your computer

Your tabs, history, Archive, settings, screenshots and Duet chats are kept only on your computer (where). There’s no Chord server.

If you turn on sync (Settings → Your data), Chord writes your setup to a folder you choose, one your own sync app (iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Syncthing) already syncs, encrypted with your passphrase before it leaves Chord: your sync app’s company can’t read it. History, open Today tabs, Duet chats, site permissions and proxies are never synced. Sync is off until you turn it on.

Importing from another browser reads that browser’s files on your computer. It never copies passwords, cookies or extensions.

A private window keeps no history, cookies or site data once it closes, and Duet never reads it.

What Chord contacts, and why

GitHub
Chord asks GitHub whether there’s a new version, 30 seconds after it starts and then once a day, and downloads updates from there. GitHub sees your IP address. Turn the check off in Settings → About.
easylist.to
Every few days, Chord refreshes the lists it blocks ads and trackers with. Pages are checked against them on your computer, so easylist.to sees only your IP address, never what you visit.
Google Safe Browsing
Chord keeps Google’s lists of dangerous sites, as short hashes, and checks addresses against them on your computer. Only when an address’s 4-byte hash prefix is in a list does Chord ask Google about that prefix, never the address. Off in Google-free mode.
Your search engine
Your searches, and, as you type one, what you’ve typed so far, for suggestions. Addresses you type are never sent. Turn suggestions off in Settings → General.
The site you’re about to open
While you type an address in the command bar, Chord opens a connection to that site a moment early, so it loads sooner. Nothing is asked for until you open it.
Google’s dictionary server
On Windows and Linux, a spelling dictionary the first time you use a language. macOS uses its own spellchecker. Not in Google-free mode.
Chrome Web Store
Extensions you choose to install, and checks for their updates. Google-free mode stops the checks.
Google Lens or Bing
Only when you search with an image, and only the image you chose.
Your Claude Code or Codex
Only if you set up Duet, which uses your own app and account: what you ask, and what Duet reads, goes to Anthropic or OpenAI under your account. You choose what Duet can read in Settings → Duet, down to nothing at all. Chord never sees your sign-in.
Chord Player
Only if you install it, for streaming services that need Widevine. It checks GitHub for its own updates, and its Widevine module comes from Google, so it isn’t offered in Google-free mode.

Google-free mode

One switch in Settings → Privacy stops Chord contacting Google on its own: no Safe Browsing, no dictionary downloads, no extension update checks, no Google search or Lens, and no Chord Player. Sites you visit can still load things from Google, and signing in to Google still works.

This website

chordbrowser.com sets no cookies and runs no analytics or third-party scripts. Cloudflare passes it to you and sees your IP address, as any web host would. Downloads come from GitHub.

Questions

Ask, or point out anything this page gets wrong, on GitHub. For a security problem, please follow the security policy instead. Chord’s code is open, so all of this can be checked.
